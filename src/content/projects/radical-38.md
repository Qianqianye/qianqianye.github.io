What are the origin stories of the words we use daily? How does language govern the way we perceive gender? Who has the right to reimagine the languages/genders/futures?

女: Radical 38 is an ongoing research-based project investing sexism embedded in the Chinese language. The project focuses on the radical [女 (nǚ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_38), which means "woman" or "female" in Chinese. The radical 女 (nǚ), a pictogram of a woman with breasts kneeling, is included in characters such as 奴 (slave), 奸 (traitor), 妒 (envy), and many others.

In the first iteration of the project, the studied characters are printed in the style of traditional calligraphy tracing book (描红字帖), with which I learned how to write as a kid. The radical 女 (nǚ) is only shown as outlined, offering the audience the chance to unlearn and relearn writing it. The reimagined character collection are currently underdevelopment through a series of participatory workshops where participants become language creators, stroke by stroke, radical by radical, character by character. All the invented characters will be archived in a dictionary, which will be opensourced later. Here includes some highlights from the first workshop participants, including invented Chinese characters for They/Them pronoun, queer, gender fluidity and Metoo 🍚🐰. More coming soon.

This piece is onview at [女 Nǚ: Other Half of The Sky](https://www.b4bel4b.com/halfofthesky) group showe at B4BEL4B from January 18 to March 7, 2020.

Radical 38 Symbol Study

![](/images/art/radical-38/qianqian-ye-woman-character.jpg)

怒 \[nù\] Rage & \[女 nǚ\] Female\
I have been doing research about Female Rage recently. Below are two surprising but pleasant learnings:\
1\. In Mandarin, the pronunciations of these two words (nù & nǚ) are quite similar.\
2\. The character 怒 (rage) is formed by three parts: 女(female), 又(again) & 心(heart), which means at old time, people might think ' rage = female heart is touched again'
